## Option1: engineered air-displacement chamber

Replace the present vented reservoir with a **purpose-built chamber designed for its declared pressure and vacuum conditions**, approved wetted materials, sludge access, reinforcement, fittings and test documentation. Retain at least125 L usable return capacity after accounting for internal structure, dead volume and operating level. A shallow rectangular package offers a better chance of staying in the rear bay than a conventional cylindrical150 L vessel. This is a fabrication-engineering quote, not an ordinary sheet-metal tank with its vent capped.

Concept air path: compressor isolation/filter → documented regulation and flow limitation → fail-closed supply valve → chamber; a separately controlled vent permits gravity return. Independent pressure protection, high-high liquid stop, pressure indication, safe vent discharge, overflow arrangement and service isolation belong in the engineered package. No automatic restart after lost air/power. Existing solenoids may be reusable only after checking voltage, flow, pressure and whether they work at the near-zero pressure differential of a low-head chamber. The venturi is not needed for gravity return and does not make an unrated tank pressure- or vacuum-rated.

The approximately0.65 m static lift corresponds to **6.38 kPa /0.925 psi** for water, ignoring pipe losses and changing levels. This is a physics check, **not an operating or relief setpoint**. Even1 psi across a900 ×500 mm flat panel produces about **3103 N /698 lbf**. Chamber flat panels, closures, welds and attachments therefore need an actual design, not a guess based on “low pressure.”

Fit constraint: the present reservoir drawing occupies roughly930 ×530 ×380 mm externally. An ideal150 L cylinder at350 mm diameter needs about1559 mm of straight cylindrical length; at500 mm diameter it needs764 mm before heads, fittings and feet, and exceeds the current380 mm reservoir height. A catalog150 L pressure vessel cannot be assumed to fit. Reducing chamber volume to make it fit cannot reduce required drain-back capacity. An independent overflow/vent arrangement must also fit; the existing overflow into a vented reservoir cannot be copied blindly into a sealed chamber design.

Cost scope: chamber engineering/fabrication/testing and regulation/protection package **quote required**. No credible supplier price for this custom fit was obtained. Existing compressor and compatible owned valves are$0 reuse; all modifications, plumbing and protective components remain additional. Keep this option outside the priced base build until a supplier supplies an approved assembly drawing and quote.

## Option2: air-operated diaphragm refill pump, vented reservoir retained

This is the more directly purchasable compressor-fed path. Keep the fixed pan, gravity drain, open vented settling reservoir and independent overflow. Replace the electric refill pump with an **air-operated double-diaphragm pump**. An air solenoid starts/stops it; there is no air connection to the reservoir headspace. Stop refill on pan high-high, reservoir low-low or changeover fault. Pickup stays above settled sludge; the pump's passage size is not a guarantee of indefinite abrasive-slurry life. Fluid-side hose/fittings must suit the selected pump's possible discharge pressure, with any pressure limiting required by the pump manufacturer; an air-gapped return still prevents siphoning.

A concrete candidate is **Graco Husky307 D32966**, polypropylene body with Santoprene wetted elastomers, subject to additive compatibility and solids screening. Manufacturer data gives6.5 GPM maximum flow,5.5 SCFM maximum air use and1/16-inch/1.588 mm maximum solids; its operating curve, inlet restrictions and actual compressor delivery determine real cycle time. The larger Husky515 can demand28 SCFM, so choosing it solely for more flow would be a poor match to the stated12 CFM compressor. Pump during changeover when plasma is idle; confirm what pressure the compressor's12 CFM rating refers to.
